# The French Perfumer — Republication Project

Simon Barbe, *The French Perfumer* (London, 1696) — English translation of
*Le Parfumeur françois* (Lyon, 1693), the first important French work on
perfume, by the most renowned perfumer of the 17th and early 18th century.

## Status

Public domain — published 1696 (French original 1693). Free to republish.

## The plan

Differentiate this edition from a plain reprint (required for KDP, which
rejects undifferentiated public-domain reprints):

- New introduction — Barbe, his shop at the sign of the Golden Fleece,
  rue des Gravilliers, Paris; perfumer to the court; the book's place as
  the first perfumer's manual.
- Modernized spelling — the 1696 text uses the long s (ſ), "oyl",
  "pomatum", "wash-balls" and the like. A modern-spelling text, with the
  original kept alongside.
- Notes — ingredients, measures and terms a modern reader won't know;
  which recipes are practical today.
- The French original (1693) as a companion reference.

## The book

[16], 126 pages, 12mo. "Teaching the several ways of extracting the odours
of drugs and flowers, and making all the compositions of perfumes for
powder, wash-balls, essences, oyls, wax, pomatum, paste, Queen of Hungary's
Rosa Solis, and other sweet waters..." Also how to colour and scent gloves
and fans, and the secret of cleansing tobacco. Written for
non-professionals, "for the Publick Good" — Barbe's professional manual
followed in 1699 as *Le Parfumeur Royal*.

## Republication check (Nov 16, 2026)

No modern commercial reprint found — only the originals in the rare-book
trade (a 1696 second edition listed at $3,000), library scans, and the
EEBO TCP scholarly transcription. The field is clear for a new edition.
